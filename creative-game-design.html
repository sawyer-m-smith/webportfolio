Two Years, One Game

Sept 2021 to Sept 2023. Unreal Engine 5.

Level 1

The problem

I'd always been drawn to game design, and I'd always quit the moment it got genuinely difficult. This project wasn't really about making a game. It was about finding out whether I could stay with something hard instead of walking away from it, the way I always had before.

Level 2

The process

I picked up Unreal Engine 5 and started with tutorials, spending hours just learning what the tools actually did before I could build anything real. Old habits showed up fast. I felt myself starting to fade the way I always had whenever a project got hard.

Boss

What went wrong

I pushed through the technical difficulty, which fixed the old problem, but created a new one: once I was good enough to keep improving the game, I couldn't figure out when to call it finished. There was always something to add, adjust, or rebuild. Knowing when to stop turned out to be its own separate skill.

Ending

What I learned

I ended up with a game I'm genuinely proud of, but the real result of this project was proving to myself that I could actually stick with something past the point where I used to quit. The persistence was the point. The game was just the evidence.
