Flipping Computers, Not Just Cars

# 2021 to 2022. Profitable, then shut down.

Overview

I knew I wanted to do something in tech but had no idea which direction, so I started at the most hands-on edge available to me: buying broken computers and making them work again.

How it worked

I sourced old computers from eBay, yard sales, and Facebook Marketplace, refurbished them with cleaning, upgraded parts, and real diagnostic work, and sold them through my own network, which grew from family and friends into friends-of-friends. Starting the business at all was the hardest part. I remember being genuinely uncomfortable putting myself out there to sell something for the first time.

Incident report

COVID hit, and computer part prices spiked right as demand for used machines dropped. A model that had been working stopped working, and no amount of effort on my end was going to fix a supply chain problem.

What I learned

The business itself turned a real profit while it lasted. More importantly, I learned that taking initiative on something uncomfortable is a skill you can build, and that knowing when to stop is just as important as knowing how to start. This ended up being the first thread that pulled me toward exploring tech careers more seriously.
